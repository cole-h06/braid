\begin{abstract}

In this paper, we present BRAID, a graph-based framework for assertion evaluation that estimates source reliability from the structure of an information network. The information network is represented as a bipartite graph, where nodes correspond to sources and claims, and edges correspond to assertions linking sources to the claims they assert. Source reliability is estimated by applying an iterative propagation algorithm in which reliability is repeatedly propagated between sources and claims until a stable reliability distribution is reached. Unique properties of the proposed approach are explored in several experiments, which include convergence analysis, the initial allocation of reliability, graph connectivity effects, claim representation, and the incorporation of agreement-weighted propagation. It is shown that reliability can be estimated directly from the graph structure while allowing additional structural information to be incorporated into the propagation process. We believe that graph-based assertion evaluation provides a promising foundation for reliability estimation within the emerging Agentic Web.

\end{abstract}
